The Cirera

An off-the-shelf development board with a screen. The engine gets the most out of it: it draws straight into strips, splits the work between the two cores and keeps whatever doesn't fit on the microSD card.

Board
Freenove FNK0104N
Chip
ESP32-S3R8, two cores at 240 MHz
Memory
512 KB of internal RAM, 8 MB of PSRAM, 16 MB of flash
Screen
3.5″, 480×320, ST77922 over QSPI, touch
Sound
ES8311 with speaker
The rest
microSD, battery, USB

Install the firmware from the browser

  1. Connect the board to your computer over USB.
  2. Press the button and choose the board's port.
  3. Wait a few minutes: it starts with 17 games in its memory.
This browser can't talk to the board over USB: open this page in Chrome or Edge on a computer. Installing requires HTTPS.

Cirera on your computer

The console as an app for Mac, Windows and Linux: its menu, the example games inside, and any game from the Library. It is the same engine, so the game is the same, with a keyboard or a gamepad.

Download for MacIntel and Apple Silicon (opens in a new tab)Download for Windows64-bit (opens in a new tab)Download for Linuxx86_64 (opens in a new tab)

Mac: macOS 11 or later, one app for Macs with Intel and with Apple Silicon · Windows 10 or later · Linux: Ubuntu 22.04, Debian 12 or later · about 9 MB

Linux on ARM, like a Raspberry Pi with its 64-bit system? Download it for ARM (opens in a new tab).

  1. Open it the first time

    It is not signed by Apple or Microsoft yet, so your system warns you the first time.

    On a Mac, unzip it, drag Cirera to Applications and open it. When it warns you, go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and click “Open Anyway”.

    On Windows, unzip it and open Cirera.exe. If “Windows protected your PC” shows up, click “More info” and then “Run anyway”.

    On Linux, unpack the .tar.gz and open Cirera, inside its folder. Installing games by dropping them on the window needs unzip, which nearly every distribution already has.

  2. Add games

    Download the .zip of any game on this page or in the Library and drop it on Cirera's window: it installs and shows up in the menu.

  3. Play

    Arrows to move; Z or Enter is A, X or Esc is B, C is C and P is START. Hold I (MENU) for two seconds to go back to the menu, and F11 for full screen. On a gamepad, X or Y act as C, and SELECT as MENU. Your saves stay on your computer.

In RetroArch and RetroPie

No console? The engine is also a libretro core: pinyol games play in RetroArch and in everything built on it, such as RetroPie or Lakka on a Raspberry Pi, Batocera, Recalbox, the handhelds that run it, or a computer. It is the same engine, so the game is the same.

  1. Install it on RetroPie

    On the Raspberry Pi, this command downloads the core already built (a Pi 1 or Zero builds it, in a few minutes) and adds Pinyol to EmulationStation.

    cd ~/RetroPie-Setup
curl -fsSL --create-dirs \
  -o ext/pinyol/scriptmodules/libretrocores/lr-pinyol.sh \
  https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ipaud/pinyol/main/platform/libretro/lr-pinyol.sh
sudo ./retropie_packages.sh lr-pinyol

  2. Copy the games

    Any game's .zip, like the ones you can download here, goes in its folder.

    ~/RetroPie/roms/pinyol/

  3. Play

    Restart EmulationStation and Pinyol shows up with its games. On the gamepad, X or Y act as C, and SELECT as MENU.

Another RetroArch? On a computer with Linux, Batocera or Lakka, the core already built (opens in a new tab) goes in RetroArch's cores folder; the guide (opens in a new tab) explains it. Tested in RetroArch and on an emulated RetroPie system, not yet on a real Raspberry Pi: if you try it, tell us how it goes (opens in a new tab).
